## 2. Brand role

UseSafeWeb is the clear, restrained identity for a **First Phone Safety Setup** service. Its role is to help an intended parent/caregiver establish sensible first-phone safeguards across three bounded layers:

1. relevant native phone safeguards;
2. the approved encrypted AdGuard-backed DNS/internet baseline; and
3. zero or one genuinely relevant external-service safeguard.

The brand is an orchestration and guidance layer around a bounded protection service. It is not a generic DNS brand, a surveillance product, a child-monitoring suite, a complete parental-control platform or a guarantee of online safety.

## 3. Intended audience — provisional, not validated

### Primary intended audience

A parent/caregiver responsible for setup around a child's first independently used smartphone transition, provisionally around the 10–12 life stage and initially oriented to the UK/England context.

### Current audience assumption

The design assumption is that this parent benefits from one coherent path that reduces the need to understand several disconnected phone, DNS and service settings while preserving clear control and limits.

This is an owner-authorized design assumption, not evidence that representative parents prefer the service, understand the terminology, complete the setup, trust the brand or perceive enough value. Public/internal wording must not turn this assumption into a research finding.

## 4. Core brand promise

### Internal promise statement

**UseSafeWeb helps you put sensible first-phone safeguards in place through one clear, privacy-respecting setup path — and tells you plainly what is protected, what still needs attention, and what is not covered.**

### What makes the promise supportable

The promise is deliberately limited to capabilities already present in accepted product/design evidence:

- native safeguards are routed first rather than falsely treated as DNS features;
- the internet baseline uses the accepted encrypted AdGuard-backed path for supported devices/conditions;
- at most one relevant service safeguard is added when applicable rather than manufacturing extra work;
- protection state distinguishes verified, parent-confirmed, action-needed, not-covered, uncertain/error and removed conditions;
- removal, recovery, troubleshooting and compatibility limits are explicit;
- the active product is accountless-first and privacy-minimal;
- routine browsing/query/activity-history surveillance is not a product capability;
- setup finishes without a mandatory login, payment or engagement loop.

### Promise boundary / non-promises

UseSafeWeb does **not** promise:

- complete online safety;
- that every harmful page, message, image, video or in-app item will be blocked;
- that DNS filtering inspects content inside an allowed domain/app/service;
- that all device/network combinations are supported;
- that a configured setting always means the effective traffic path is currently protected;
- monitoring, recording or reporting a child's browsing/activity;
- emergency response, safeguarding intervention or replacement of parental/native/service safeguards;
- that setup is effortless, foolproof, impossible to bypass or proven with representative parents;
- legal/compliance certification or public-market readiness.

## 5. Brand idea and strategic positioning

### Brand idea

**Clear guardrails for safer first-phone independence.**

This expresses the frozen `SAFE INDEPENDENCE` framing without claiming complete safety. “Guardrails” means bounded safeguards with visible limits, not surveillance or absolute control.

### Positioning statement — internal

For a parent/caregiver setting up a child's first independently used smartphone, UseSafeWeb is a focused first-phone safety setup service that brings native safeguards, a privacy-conscious DNS protection baseline and one relevant service safeguard into one truthful setup journey. It differentiates the product **within its own design** by being accountless-first, non-surveillance, explicit about verification/coverage and designed for safe removal/recovery. This is not a market-superiority or uniqueness claim.

## 6. Differentiation pillars

These are product-grounded design differentiators, not comparative market claims.

| Pillar | Brand meaning | Required proof/boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **One coherent first-phone path** | Phone, Internet and relevant Service safeguards are presented as one bounded setup journey. | Do not imply all safety controls are technically provided by UseSafeWeb/DNS. |
| **Truth before apparent completion** | Protected/verified, parent-confirmed, action-needed, not-covered, uncertain/error and removed states remain distinct. | Never promote a configured/parent-confirmed or synthetic state to verified. |
| **Privacy-respecting by design** | Accountless-first, no browsing/query/activity-history product, no child behavioral profile, minimal transient journey state. | Do not claim “zero data”, “anonymous” or equivalent absolute privacy language unless separately proven for the exact context. |
| **Non-surveillance** | The service establishes guardrails without covert monitoring or a child-activity dashboard. | Do not describe UseSafeWeb as child monitoring, tracking, message inspection or browsing reporting. |
| **Clear limits** | Unsupported, uncertain and not-covered states are visible rather than hidden. | Never convert uncertainty into a green/verified state for marketing simplicity. |
| **Control and reversibility** | Supported paths include removal/recovery and restoration guidance. | Do not imply lock-in, unremovability or bypass-proof enforcement. |
| **Self-service orientation** | Prevention, automatic checks where privacy-safe, issue-specific help, bounded AI assistance and recovery precede exceptional human escalation. | Do not promise routine staffed support or fabricate an escalation route. |

## 7. Trust strategy

Trust should be earned by observable product behavior and accurate language rather than fear, authority theatre or surveillance claims.

### Trust signals to emphasize

1. **Say exactly what the service does.** Describe the three bounded layers and DNS scope plainly.
2. **Show evidence state.** A protection claim is tied to its actual evidence class; uncertainty stays visible.
3. **Show limits before failure.** Compatibility, VPN/alternate-DNS/Private-Relay/custom-resolver/network limitations remain accessible.
4. **Respect parental control.** The parent can stop, recover, remove and restore normal DNS behavior through the supported path.
5. **Minimize data and identity.** No mandatory account or persistent child/family profile is required for the active journey.
6. **Separate safeguard layers.** Native/service settings are not represented as UseSafeWeb technical verification unless they really are verified.
7. **Avoid manufactured urgency.** No fear-based countdown, shame, guilt, “your child is unsafe” pressure or dark pattern is part of the brand system.
8. **Keep help truthful.** If a condition is unsupported or unresolved, say so rather than presenting speculative instructions or a fake success state.

## 8. Brand personality

The provisional personality is defined as behavioral design rules, not a claim that parents have validated these traits.

### 8.1 Calm

Use measured language. Do not sensationalize online risk or imply imminent harm to force action.

### 8.2 Clear

Prefer ordinary words, short explanations, concrete next actions and explicit scope. Explain technical terms only when they are needed to complete or understand a step.

### 8.3 Respectful

Address the parent as a capable decision-maker. Do not shame, frighten or infantilize the parent or child.

### 8.4 Protective, not controlling

Frame safeguards as enabling safer independence, not as covert observation or total control of the child.

### 8.5 Truthful

Use uncertainty, limitations and not-covered states when the evidence requires them. Never choose reassuring copy over evidence truth.

### 8.6 Practical

Prioritize the next useful action, minimal choices and recovery/removal. Avoid unnecessary feature language, engagement hooks or technical performance theatre.

### 8.7 Quietly trustworthy

Trust comes from clear limits, privacy restraint, supportable claims and reversibility. Avoid badges, certifications, statistics or endorsements without current evidence.

## 9. Voice principles for later TSK-0299

The verbal system should inherit these constraints:

- parent-facing, plain-language and non-alarmist;
- child-aware without talking about the child as a threat, data source or surveillance target;
- concise but not so simplified that protection limits disappear;
- direct about unsupported/uncertain conditions;
- no jargon-led identity such as “DNS security stack” in the primary proposition;
- no false personalization or account language in an accountless journey;
- no legal/compliance wording beyond current approved source material;
- no “validated by parents” wording until the required real evidence exists;
- terminology must remain compatible with the source-backed/localizable content architecture.

## 10. Prohibited expression and claim register

The following are prohibited unless a later authoritative evidence/gate change explicitly allows a narrower factual statement.

| Prohibited expression/claim class | Examples that must not be used | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Complete-safety claims** | “Complete protection”, “100% safe online”, “blocks everything harmful”, “total internet safety” | DNS/service scope is bounded and accepted claims rules prohibit total protection. |
| **Surveillance/control claims** | “See everything your child does”, “monitor browsing”, “track your child”, “read messages”, “full parental control” | Contradicts frozen non-surveillance/current product scope. |
| **False verification** | “Protected” based only on setup presence, parent confirmation, old evidence or synthetic rehearsal | Verification must match current evidence state. |
| **Bypass-proof/permanence** | “Impossible to bypass”, “always protected”, “cannot be removed” | Network/device conditions can change and supported removal is an explicit requirement. |
| **Absolute privacy** | “We collect nothing”, “zero data”, “completely anonymous” | Current architecture is privacy-minimal, but absolute statements require exact separate proof. |
| **False legal/compliance authority** | “Legally approved”, “certified safe”, “fully compliant”, “GDPR certified” | Legal completion remains deferred/unresolved; no certification is established by current evidence. |
| **Behavioral-validation claims** | “Parents love it”, “proven easy for parents”, “validated with families”, “parents understand it” | `RSK-0002` remains OPEN; representative-parent validation has not occurred. |
| **Unsupported market superiority** | “The safest”, “the only”, “best parental protection”, “unique” | No current comparative market evidence is established by this task. |
| **Fear/shame pressure** | “Your child is in danger unless…”, “Responsible parents must…”, countdown/urgent threat framing | Conflicts with calm, respectful, non-alarmist posture and creates coercive trust. |
| **False readiness** | “Launch-ready”, “available everywhere”, “works on every device/network”, “fully supported” | Device/network, legal, participant, publication and launch gates remain bounded. |
| **Routine human-support promise** | “24/7 support team”, “a specialist is always available” | Routine staffed support is not in the current operating model. |
| **Payment/value claim** | “Premium protection”, “pay for better safety”, “guaranteed value” | Core protection remains free and payment/supporter activation is separately gated. |

## 12. Visual-direction implications for TSK-0302 / TSK-0301

This strategy does not select a logo, color palette, typography family, icon family or final visual identity. It constrains later visual directions:

- favor clarity, calm, legibility and accessible contrast over alarm imagery or cyber-security spectacle;
- avoid surveillance motifs such as eyes, cameras, target reticles, tracking maps or child-location imagery;
- avoid militarized/absolute-security motifs that imply invulnerability, such as impenetrable shields paired with total-safety claims;
- avoid childish styling that undermines parent trust or frames the product as a toy;
- avoid clinical/legal authority theatre and unsupported certification badges;
- allow protective/guardrail metaphors only where they do not imply complete protection;
- preserve accessible, responsive and RTL-capable design-system constraints for later implementation.

The HUMAN_ONLY TSK-0301 owner decision remains the authority for final visual identity selection. TSK-0298 does not pre-approve any identity direction.
